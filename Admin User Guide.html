Admin User Guide

Everything the hospital team needs to keep the Nemocnica Snina website accurate — departments, clinics, physicians, booking rules, news and official disclosures — without touching a line of code.

For editors, clinicians & administrators Bilingual SK / EN Changes go live immediately
01

About this guide

The content admin is the control room for the public website. From one screen you can add a new physician, change a clinic's opening hours, publish an announcement, or upload a contract for the disclosures register — and every change appears on the live site within moments.

This guide is written for non-technical staff. You do not need any web or IT background. If you can fill in a form, you can run the website.

The three things to remember

  1. You are editing the live site. There is no separate "draft website" for Slovak and English — when you press Save, the public page updates. Read your text before saving.
  2. Slovak is the original; English sits beside it. Most content fields show two boxes — SK and EN — side by side. Fill in both so every visitor gets a complete page.
  3. Booking rules are clinical safety settings. The fields that control which days and times patients can book are covered in their own section. Treat them with the same care as a real appointment book.

Who can do whatYour account has a role — editor, clinician, or administrator — and some areas are limited to certain roles. If a button or section described here is missing for you, it is restricted to another role. See Roles & permissions.

02

Signing in & security

The admin lives at the /admin address of the hospital website. Access is protected by your staff account and a second security step, because the system can reach personal data and clinical settings.

First sign-in — set up your authenticator

Every staff account uses two-factor authentication (MFA). The first time you log in, you connect an authenticator app on your phone (such as the one issued by hospital IT). After that, each login asks for a fresh 6-digit code.

  1. Open the authenticator app on your phone.
  2. On the admin login screen, scan the QR code shown (or type the setup key) to add "Nemocnica Snina Admin" to your app.
  3. Enter the 6-digit code from the app to confirm. Your phone is now your second key.
  4. Store the recovery codes you are given somewhere safe — they let you back in if you lose your phone.

03

The interface

Once you are in, the screen has two parts: a dark blue rail on the left for navigation, and the working area on the right where you read and edit.

The left rail

The rail is grouped into three sets:

  • Content — the seven things you edit most: Departments, Clinics, Physicians, Services, Diagnostics, News and Disclosures. Each shows a live count of how many items it holds.
  • Settings — two "single page" areas: Hospital info (address, phone numbers, IDs) and Page content (the home-page banner and intro text).
  • Tools — Export, Import and Reset, used for backups. See Tools & backups.

The top bar

Along the top of the working area you'll find:

  • An SK / EN switch — changes the language of the admin labels only. It does not change which language you are editing; both languages are always edited together.
  • View website — opens the public site in a new tab so you can check your changes.
  • Sign out — always use this when you finish.

Keep the website open in a second tabUse View website before you start. After each save, switch to that tab and refresh to see exactly what visitors see.

04

How editing works

Every content type works the same way, so once you've learned one you've learned them all. There are three screens: the list, the editor, and a small confirmation message.

The list

Choosing a content type from the rail shows its list — one row per item, each with an icon, a title, a short summary, and a status badge. On the right of each row are two buttons: a pencil to edit and a bin to delete. The blue Add new button (top right) creates a fresh item.

Saving

  1. Make your changes in the form.
  2. Press the blue Save button at the bottom. A small "Saved" message confirms it worked.
  3. The list reappears and the public site is already updated. Use Cancel instead if you want to discard changes.

Web addresses are created for youWhen you add a new item, the system builds its web address automatically from the title. You don't need to set it. (Disclosures are the one exception — there you type the document number yourself.)

Managing content

A note on links between items

Departments, clinics & facilities are connected to physicians

You don't list a department's doctors inside the department. Instead, each physician record points to its department (and clinic or facility). The website then assembles the "related physicians" list automatically. So to move a doctor between wards, you edit the doctor — not the ward.

07

Booking rules — read carefully

These five clinic fields control the online appointment wizard. A mistake here doesn't just look wrong — it can offer patients appointments that don't exist, or hide ones that do. Change them only when you are sure, and check the live booking flow afterwards.

Treat these like the real appointment bookIf unsure, ask the clinic's lead nurse or physician before changing booking weekdays, the booking window, or the status. After any change, open View website → book a test appointment to confirm the right days and times appear.

1 · Status

The status dropdown sets the badge patients see and whether booking is possible:

Open New · accepting Temporary measures Closed

A clinic set to Temporary measures or Closed will not accept online bookings, even if other fields say otherwise. Use these when a clinic is paused.

2 · Online booking switch

The master switch. If it's off, the clinic shows a greyed-out "Online booking unavailable" button and no wizard — regardless of the days or window. Turn it on only for clinics that genuinely take online appointments.

3 · Booking weekdays

This decides which days of the week the wizard offers. Days are entered as numbers, separated by commas, using this key:

Mo1
Tu2
We3
Th4
Fr5
Sa6
Su0

So Monday is 1, Tuesday 2 … Saturday 6, and Sunday is 0. To let patients book on Tuesdays and Thursdays only, you'd enter 2, 4. The wizard will then offer only the next available Tuesdays and Thursdays.

4 · Booking window

An optional time range that narrows bookings to part of the day, written like 13:00–14:00. Leave it blank to offer the clinic's normal slots; fill it in when a clinic only books within a fixed hour.

5 · Referral required

When this switch is on, the patient must tick a box confirming they hold a referral (výmenný lístok) before they can finish booking. Use it for clinics that legally require one.

Always write the plain-language booking rule tooWhen you set days or a window, also fill the Booking rule field (SK + EN) with a sentence patients can understand — e.g. "Bookings Thu & Fri, 13:00–14:00; a referral is required." The numbers drive the system; this sentence reassures the patient.

Images & PDFs

Where a page shows a photo (department, facility, map) or a document (a disclosure PDF), the editor gives you an upload field. Until a real file is added, the site shows a neutral striped placeholder labelled with what belongs there.

  1. In the editor, find the image or file field and choose Upload.
  2. Select the file from your computer, or pick one already in the media library to reuse it.
  3. For photos, add a short description (alt text) — this is read aloud by screen readers and is required for accessibility.
  4. Save. The placeholder is replaced by your file.
UseBest formatGuidance
Department / facility photosJPGLandscape, good light. Aim for a wide 16:9 shot; large images are resized for you.
Maps / diagramsPNG or JPGMake sure text in the image is legible at small sizes.
Disclosure documentsPDFThe final signed version. Name the file clearly before uploading.

Use real photos before launchEvery placeholder on the live site should be replaced with a genuine photograph. Avoid generic stock imagery where a real photo of the hospital would build more trust.

14

Translations & the review gate

The site is built for six languages: Slovak and English first, then Czech, Polish, Hungarian and Ukrainian. Slovak is always the human-written original. English is written by staff. The other four are produced by machine translation — and that's where the review step matters.

How the languages flow

  • Slovak (SK) — you write it. This is the source of truth.
  • English (EN) — you write it in the box beside Slovak.
  • Czech / Polish / Hungarian / Ukrainian — generated automatically from your Slovak, using a locked glossary so medical and department terms stay correct.

Machine translations are never published automaticallyFor clinical and safety-related content (departments, clinics, services, news), the four machine-translated languages arrive as drafts marked "needs review". A person must read and approve each one before it goes live. This is a safety rule — do not bypass it.

Reviewing a translation

  1. Open the item and switch to the language marked needs review.
  2. Read the machine translation against the Slovak original. Check medical terms, names and numbers especially.
  3. Correct anything that reads awkwardly or wrongly.
  4. Mark it approved / publish. Only then does that language appear on the public site.

If you're not confident in a language, leave it in "needs review" and ask a colleague who is. An unreviewed page simply stays hidden in that language — which is safer than publishing a mistranslation.

Hospital info & page content

The two items under Settings aren't lists — they're single pages you edit in place.

Hospital info

Name, address, IDs and the phone numbers used across the whole site

This holds the details that appear in the footer, contact page and emergency card: official name, tagline, address, registration numbers (IČO / DIČ), switchboard, reception, pharmacy, email and region. Change a phone number here once and it updates everywhere.

Page content

The editable text blocks on the home and contact pages

Three grouped blocks, each bilingual:

  • Hero banner — the badge, headline and subtitle at the top of the home page.
  • About — the introductory text about the hospital.
  • Emergency (APS) — the "who's on duty" notice. Note: once the live regional feed is connected, this text becomes a fallback shown only if that feed is unavailable.

Tools & backups

The Tools group holds three actions. The first two are safe; the third is not.

ToolWhat it doesWhen to use it
Export dataDownloads a complete copy of all content as a single file.Before any big editing session, as a safety copy. Keep it somewhere safe.
Import dataLoads content back from a previously exported file.To restore from a backup, or move content between environments. It replaces current content.
Reset to defaultsWipes all your changes and returns to the original sample content.Almost never. This is destructive.

"Reset to defaults" erases everything you've enteredIt cannot be undone except by importing a backup. Export first, and only reset if you genuinely want to start over. When in doubt, don't — ask an administrator.

17

Roles & permissions

Not everyone sees the same buttons. Your account has one of three roles, and the system hides what your role isn't meant to touch.

RoleCan do
EditorAdd and edit content — departments, clinics, physicians, services, facilities, news, disclosures, page text. The day-to-day website work.
ClinicianEverything an editor can, plus the patient-onboarding review queue (accepting or rejecting new-patient applications).
AdministratorEverything, plus account management, the data tools, and the GDPR data-request tools (exporting or erasing a person's data on request).

Every action you take — create, edit, delete, or a sensitive data access — is written to a permanent audit log with your name, the time, and what changed. This protects both patients and staff, and is a legal requirement.

18

Good practices

  • Fill both languages. A page with Slovak but no English looks half-finished to an English visitor. Complete SK and EN together.
  • Check the live page after saving. Switch to your "View website" tab and refresh. Trust your eyes, not just the form.
  • Export before big changes. A 10-second backup can save an afternoon.
  • Keep titles short and clear. They become menu items and card headings.
  • Double-check booking fields. Days, window, status. When in doubt, test a booking.
  • Write for patients. Plain, calm, reassuring language — avoid internal abbreviations the public won't know.
  • Sign out on shared computers. Your account is your responsibility.
19

Troubleshooting

My change isn't showing on the website

First make sure you pressed Save and saw the "Saved" message. Then refresh the public page (the site may hold a short cache — wait a minute and refresh again). Confirm you're looking at the right language on the public site.

I can't see a section this guide mentions

It's probably limited to another role. The onboarding queue is for clinicians and administrators; the data tools and GDPR tools are administrator-only. Ask an administrator if you need access.

I deleted something by mistake

If you have a recent Export file, an administrator can Import it to restore. This is exactly why exporting before big edits is worth the habit.

The booking wizard offers the wrong days

Open the clinic and re-check Booking weekdays (Mon=1 … Sun=0), the window, the status, and the Online booking switch. See Booking rules.

I lost my phone / can't get my code

Use one of the recovery codes you saved at first sign-in. If you don't have them, contact hospital IT to reset your authenticator — for security, this can't be done from inside the admin.

Still stuck?For content questions, ask your site administrator. For login, access, or technical problems, contact hospital IT. Keep this guide handy — most answers are a section away.
